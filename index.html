Method

Rendering from the game state

Given where a player stands and looks, what everyone in the round is doing, and the map being played, the model renders what that player sees. Map structure, lighting and materials stay consistent as the camera moves through rooms and across levels.

Map-aware

Each frame is conditioned on per-pixel ray casts against the map geometry, so walls, floors and sky stay where the map puts them.

Full game state

The camera pose, the player's own inputs and the state and actions of all ten players enter as separate conditions.

Seven maps

Ancient, Anubis, Dust II, Inferno, Mirage, Nuke and Overpass, at 720p and 32 fps.

Video backbone

Fine-tuned from Cosmos3-Nano with the Wan2.2 VAE; an eight-second window is denoised jointly.

Multi-view

Several players, one moment

All players in a round share one game state. Rendering each of their views from that shared state should give pictures that agree: the same wall, the same opponent, seen through different eyes.

What is aligned

  • StarCraft II: each replay re-run in its own engine build with fog of war, once per player; observations, unit commands and camera moves per game loop.
  • Dota 2: engine state deltas and the full message stream, with a video and a frame-to-tick table for every player slot.
  • Counter-Strike 2: first-person video per round and player, with per-tick player state, inputs and events.
